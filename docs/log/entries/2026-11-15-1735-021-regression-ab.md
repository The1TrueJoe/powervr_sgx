# 0.2.1 against 0.2.0 on a CE5300 board, and two switches for a Cedarview slowdown

2026-11-15, a CE5300 board, RAM boots of the v0.2 release kernel and a test build of m2-drm with
`cpu_latency_us`, the open microkernel; Xorg (modesetting on simpledrm, glamor through kmsro) in a
Debian root. A tester reports every game slower under X on a Cedarview laptop after 0.2.1 (issue #5):
his QuakeSpasm stats show the GPU taking two to three times as long a render (render latency 38 to
124 ms on 0.2.0, 76 to 264 ms on 0.2.1) with the game's CPU idle and its draws unchanged.

- **The released packages, swapped as the root's Mesa** (Xorg and the game both on it), Minecraft
  1.7.10's title screen at 720x480 with `SGX_DEBUG=stats`, 2 minutes each, in the order 0.2.1, 0.2.0,
  0.2.1: 9.5 and 9.3 FPS on 0.2.1 at 21.6 ms a render, 5.4 FPS on 0.2.0 (its copies of the back
  buffer into textures went through the CPU). 0.2.1 is faster here; nothing in its Mesa slows the GPU
  on this board.
- **Xorg's own stats** (`SGX_DEBUG=stats` in the server) with the game running: no GPU or CPU blits on
  either version, and one render a game frame on both (11 a 2 s at 5.4 FPS, 20 at 9.6 FPS). glamor
  does not send the new GPU texture blits here, and X's jobs a frame did not change.
- **`SGX_DEBUG=notexblits`** (blits into a texture through the CPU, as 0.2.0 did): `sgx_copyleak -x
  -c 7 200` makes 1400 GPU blits without it and 1372 CPU blits (23.9 s of CPU) with it; every
  `sgx_gamefix` part passes either way, and its 4 GPU blits into render targets stay on the GPU.
- **`powervr_sgx.cpu_latency_us`** (m2-drm): `/dev/cpu_dma_latency` sampled every 20 ms through a
  surfaceless run and after it. The default (-1) keeps this core's own setting, no limit; 50 holds
  50 us while the jobs run and lets go after; 0 sets none.

One job timeout in each `sgx_gamefix` run, in `bigfbo`'s 2048x1200 frames (a second a frame): the
parameter-buffer loss of large targets (2026-11-15-1630-pb-leak-large-targets), with Xorg keeping the
GPU awake so the PB isn't re-armed between processes. No page faults, WARNs or BUGs.
