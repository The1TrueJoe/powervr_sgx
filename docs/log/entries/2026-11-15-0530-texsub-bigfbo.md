# Sub-rect texture uploads, and 2048x1200 FBO frames against the job timeout

2026-11-15, the CE5310 board, RAM boot of the v0.2.1 CE5300 kernel (release/v0.2
b5e8b2b4) with v0.2.1's open microkernel (ukernel-b5e8b2b); the kernel image and
modules md5-checked against the artifact; the normal boot restored after. Mesa
x86_64-gl from dev (the "base", before these changes) and from
wip/texsub-bigfbo; `mesa/tests/sgx_gamefix`, surfaceless.

## Sub-rect uploads (issue #5: QuakeSpasm's lightmaps, up to 443 ms of 2 s)

- A 128x16 update of an idle 128x128 RGBA8 texture: 0.117 ms on dev's Mesa,
  0.048 to 0.061 ms with the 4-byte texel copied inline instead of a
  `memcpy` call of a run-time size each. Every texel checked, under renders
  that read the texture too (the second copy).
- Building whole twiddle tiles from the CPU copy, so that no texel is written
  to the write-combined heap alone, made updates slower (more texels encoded),
  so the scattered writes weren't the cost: not kept.
- A test that draws between every update measures the GPU wait instead (both
  copies busy, `sgx_finish`); QuakeSpasm's stats show no such waits.

## 2048x1200 FBOs (issue #5: danilolc's off-screen benchmark)

- 60 frames of 30 blended full-target quads, each waited for: about 1 s a frame
  at 1448x848 and at 2048x1200. At 2048x1200 the slowest frame took 2,030 to
  2,035 ms, past powervr_sgx's 2 s `job_timeout_ms`, and the GPU was reset once
  a run; no page faults, and the frames after drew.
- So the timeouts are renders that take longer than the timeout, not a hang. A
  longer default timeout (kernel), or Mesa submitting heavy frames in parts,
  would avoid them; `powervr_sgx.job_timeout_ms=5000` works around it now.
